Ugali Recipe

Description

Ugali is a thick, dense starch porridge made from finely ground white cornmeal (maize flour) and water. It serves as the ultimate staple carbohydrate across the African Great Lakes region and Southern Africa, functioning as the national dish of both Kenya and Tanzania. Because it is unseasoned, ugali is designed to complement intensely flavorful sides. It acts as a neutral canvas for rich meat stews, fish, beans, or sukuma wiki (braised collard greens or kale).

Ingredients

  • White cornmeal (also called maize flour) — 2 cups
  • Water — 3 cups

Steps

  1. Boil the water: Bring 3 cups of water to a rolling boil in a heavy-bottomed pot over high heat.
  2. Add initial flour: Reduce the heat to medium and stir in about 1 cup of the white cornmeal, whisking quickly to prevent lumps from forming.
  3. Thicken the mixture: Let the mixture boil for 2 to 3 minutes until it starts to bubble and thicken into a porridge-like consistency.
  4. Add remaining flour: Gradually add the remaining 1 to 2 cups of cornmeal while stirring continuously and vigorously with a sturdy wooden spoon.
  5. Mash and knead: Press the dough repeatedly against the sides of the pot with the back of the wooden spoon to break up any dry pockets of flour and create a smooth, stiff texture.
  6. Steam and cook: Gather the dough into a cohesive ball in the center of the pot, reduce the heat to low, cover the pot with a lid, and let it steam for 5 to 10 minutes until it detaches from the sides and you smell a faint, roasted corn aroma.
  7. Shape and serve: Turn the hot ugali out onto a serving plate, use a wet spoon or clean wet hands to smooth it into a round dome, and serve immediately.
